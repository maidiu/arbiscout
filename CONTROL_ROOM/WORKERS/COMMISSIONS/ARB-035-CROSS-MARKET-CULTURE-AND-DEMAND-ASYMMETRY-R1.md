## GOVERNING PRODUCT VS STUDIED ECONOMIC PHENOMENON

ArbiScout itself is **exclusively a purchase-research index**: it identifies presently obtainable retailer purchase prices maximally below normal actual historical completed-sale prices on eBay for **exactly the same product/variant/condition/quantity**. It does NOT simulate subsequent transactions, predict a sale, model seller fees/ROI, or become a reseller operation.

This Rabochii may nevertheless investigate the user's *sector-level economic-cultural question*: where do large, recurrent, sufficiently reliable observed purchase-price gaps coincide with high actual historic eBay turnover, meaningful unit purchase-value, consistent real retail promotions, and plausible informational asymmetry between eBay's habitual buying communities and people monitoring retailer clearance? **Do not introduce speculative reselling, sales-fee, expected profit or inventory forecasts as active ArbiScout engineering features.** Distinguish an observed price gap from realizable profit. Practical purchase frictions are observational suitability factors, not forecasts of the user's future business.

## CORE RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS — THE CHANNEL-ATTENTION DISJUNCTION

The potentially high-yield category is not simply "high price" or "high eBay popularity." Test the **intersection** of:
A. **eBay marketplace demand:** a deep, repetitive historical completed-sales market for the same exact SKU; many ordinary-price transactions, not rare outlier highs.
B. **Retail discount cadence:** genuine, repeatable, readily discovered retailer purchase offers periodically priced far below normal eBay completed-sale levels; not a promotional MSRP illusion.
C. **Ticket size and absolute gap:** sufficient *absolute dollars* (not only percent discount) to make discovery/verification effort worthwhile, while ensuring purchase-side costs, unit size, stock and product quality do not nullify the apparent gap.
D. **Platform/cultural search asymmetry:** the eBay-side buying population searches habitual specialist venues or seeks precise replacement/collectible items, while retailer sale information circulates through partly different channels; people on one channel may not monitor the other. Must empirically evaluate this overlap; **buyer ignorance, demographics or identity cannot be assumed**.
E. **Competitive correction/persistence:** how quickly price gaps are discovered, undercut, exhausted or erased by seller/consumer comparison services, automated monitors and opportunistic purchasing.
F. **Exact matching:** standard models/part numbers/condition and historical sold data must enable demonstrable comparable prices rather than mixed models, used/new, bundles, warranty or counterfeit ambiguity.

Do not infer that enthusiasts lack price knowledge. They might be unusually expert and already follow community deals. The relevant cause, if any, could be **attention allocation, category specialization, urgency, SKU obsolescence, geography, retailer-catalog navigation costs, social community separation, search frictions, price-discrimination mechanisms, or asymmetric notification tooling**. Investigate competing causal stories.

## COMPETING HYPOTHESES TO DISCRIMINATE

1. **High-frequency familiar electronics:** high comparability and regular eBay sales, but extremely price-transparent/high competition and shallow persistent gaps.
2. **OEM automotive and repair components:** strong exact part-number/fitment search, urgent replacement needs and eBay audience, possibly distributed retailer/warehouse discounts; incompatible fitment, returns and counterfeit risk might destroy signal quality.
3. **Professional tools and replaceable batteries:** recurring brand/model demand and retailer seasonal discount cadence; expertise/deal awareness might be high.
4. **Photography/optics, audio/music, pro-AV, networking and laboratory/industrial accessories:** high ticket, identifiable models and specialist buyer communities; often low volume, warranty/condition ambiguity, missing GTIN or discontinued variants.
5. **LEGO, collectible games/cards, out-of-print media, enthusiast hobby items:** strong eBay communities and SKU specificity; hype spikes, low stock, single-sale outliers and high community deal awareness complicate conclusions.
6. **Small appliance/repair parts and discontinued branded components:** high search specificity and occasional inelastic replacement demand; sparse realized sale history and source fragmentation.
7. **Premium outdoor/sport gear, watches/luxury, footwear/fashion:** larger price points and active eBay markets; sizing, authenticity, returns/condition and seasonal discount patterns can dominate.
8. **Printer supplies, high-value consumables and accessory cartridges:** repeat purchase and retailer promotions; expiry/counterfeits/warranty constraints.
9. **Regional/local clearance and warehouse clubs:** localized discount information may be hard for distant eBay audiences to monitor; price/stock may be inaccessible or non-shippable.
10. **Negative controls:** categories with frequent dramatic percent-off MSRP but weak completed-sale history, near-identical models, huge shipping/warranty risks, or eBay prices tracking retail promotions immediately.

**These are testable candidates, not recommendations or established market winners.** Discover additional categories/industries outside this list and supply disconfirming examples.

## SOURCE PRIORITY AND RESEARCH METHOD

A. **Primary eBay marketplace evidence**: eBay 2025 Form 10-K (2026 filed) identifies focus categories including Motors P&A, collectibles, refurbished, apparel/luxury/sneakers, but aggregate eBay growth is not evidence of SKU-level price gaps or audience ignorance. eBay 2026 Recommerce survey reports channel preferences and recurring secondhand engagement, but survey-of-secondhand buyer frequencies is not exact new-in-box retail-to-eBay sale-price evidence. eBay Seller Hub Product Research offers up to 3 years of transaction prices/volume/trends; Sourcing Insights reports category demand vs supply for eligible accounts, **not a public API**. Investigate qualification/coverage and limits.
B. **Trade/retailer primary evidence:** official product/clearance/catalog API/feeds from ARB-020, annual product/sale cycles and promotion calendars, model/UPC manufacturer databases, authorized historical retail price records, public warranty and shipping/size data.
C. **Cultural and behavioral evidence:** peer-reviewed search cost, platform multi-homing, consumer price discovery, enthusiast community market participation and selective attention research; relevant public category forums and Reddit threads can generate hypotheses but are biased, nonrepresentative and NOT evidence of whole-population lack of retailer awareness. Research community-specific deal monitoring vs eBay purchase patterns where direct comparable signals exist; no demographic/class/age stereotyping.
D. **Independent datasets and methods:** appropriately licensed clickstream/referral/search trend panels or direct surveys/structured interviews ONLY if legally available, with explicit sampling/privacy caveats. Traffic proxies (Google Trends, Similarweb, newsletter feeds, deal-thread mentions) are NOT individual-level shopper overlap and cannot prove non-overlap. Avoid ecological inference.
E. **Competing marketplace signals:** specialist platforms, Amazon direct retail/third-party, Walmart etc may equilibrate prices; a gap from published eBay historical prices may reflect stale/composition-biased samples, accepted-offer display, shifts in model generation, shipping or authenticity differences.
F. **Negative and falsification cases:** abundant retail sales but few eBay completed sales; high eBay sales without real retailer discounts; high ticket but price transparent/return risk; apparent niche demand dominated by one-off collector items; seasonal boom misrepresented as year-round demand; specialized audience actually uses Slickdeals/Keepa/Discord and tracks drops faster than general buyers.

Quantities to propose and independently justify:
- **eBay demand regularity:** actual same-product completed sales per 7/30/90 days, fraction of weeks with >0 completed sales, temporal gaps, variant exactness, sold-price median/p25/p75, sample size, buyer/seller concentration where permitted, seasonal volatility. Annual category-wide GMV cannot substitute for model-level counts.
- **Retail promotion regularity:** number of verified price dips per SKU/retailer/month/season, depth relative to **ordinary eBay completed-sale reference** (not MSRP), actual available units/stock, promotion duration, geography/coupon/membership conditions, false-offer rates.
- **High-value purchase gap:** absolute dollars `eBay_regular_history_price - obtainable_retail_purchase_price`, positive percent, and **conservative** `eBay_p25 - retailer_total_purchase_price`; sensitivity to accepted-offer price uncertainty, shipping and sparse observations.
- **Detection efficiency:** verified qualifying opportunities per 100/1000 scanned offers, per source dollar, and per unit of retrieval/validation effort, net of duplicates/false matches.
- **Attention/channel asymmetry:** only measured with appropriate buyer cross-channel evidence; if using indirect proxies, publish uncertainty bounds and alternative explanations. Do not claim "eBay buyers don't use Best Buy/clearance" without actual evidence. Probe time-to-diffusion of a retailer sale into eBay reference prices/enthusiast deal communities as a possible *falsifiable* proxy.
- **Practical encumbrances:** weight/volume, shipping cost/fragility, battery/hazmat restrictions, age/expiry, serial-number/fitment, warranties, authenticity, geographic pickup, purchase limits, product condition and legal restrictions. These affect the fidelity of the purchase-gap opportunity, not a future transaction prediction.
- **Time-persistence and discovery asymmetry:** does the gap survive more than hours/days, and is it repeatable at scale? If absolute gap is large but SKU has only one completed sale in 90 days, classify as speculative.
- **Buyer intensity vs reachable base:** distinguish a small expert niche with habitual eBay transactions from a broad high-volume commodity audience; avoid presuming more subcultural specialization automatically means more demand.
- **Capital/significance proxy:** include ticket bands (e.g., <$50, $50-150, $150-300, $300-750, $750+) as *stratification to discover* rather than dogmatic winners; measure absolute-gap dollars and recurrence with uncertainty. Not a live software ROI algorithm.

**Do not collapse indicators into an arbitrary single "expected profit" score**. Provide Pareto frontier and weighted illustrative scenarios with transparently varied assumptions; distinguish high observed dollar-gap, high regularity, high scan yield and high confidence. Explicitly flag where private consumer overlap data are unavailable and propose feasible studies rather than guessing.
